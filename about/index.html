in one breathAdjie Brotosukmono is an Indonesian senior economics consultant and power system engineer at Mott MacDonald in Perth, Western Australia, and a Professional Member of Engineers Australia. Adjie works on techno-economic modelling, electricity market and dispatch modelling, power system studies and project management for miners, system operators and renewable developers, mostly in Western Australia under the WEM Rules and the Pilbara Network Rules. Adjie holds a Bachelor's in Electrical Power Engineering and a Master's in Electrical Engineering from Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB). Before Perth, Adjie taught physics online at Quipper and Pahamify, was a credit card product manager at Bank Central Asia (BCA), and was a power system engineer at Merz Consultancy, which merged into Mott MacDonald. Adjie writes at the Adjie Brotot Blog and builds free calculators at Adjie Brotools.

Stop 1 · Bandung, Indonesia

Electrical power engineering at ITB

It started with physics. Since junior high, I loved playing with formulas. Engineering felt like the obvious next step.

I went to Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB) and stayed for two degrees: a Bachelor's in Electrical Power Engineering and a Master's in Electrical Engineering. Both were funded by scholarships. I am still grateful for that.

My master's research asked a very practical question: how do you schedule coal power plants when coal deliveries are the bottleneck? It became a paper, Optimal Scheduling Algorithm for Coal-Powered Power Plants, Considering Coal Delivery Constraints.

I lived in the labs too. I coordinated the assistants of the power system engineering lab, guided students through power system analysis and high voltage practicals, and helped teach signals and systems, electrical materials and electromagnetics.

One thing at a time was never enough, so I also started a business. Ganeshtore sold books, tools and accessories to new students. We had a small team, a CFO included, and a cash flow and point-of-sale system I built on Google Sheets and Apps Script. It kept going, I learned a lot, and part of the profit went to charity.

Stop 2 · Online classrooms

Teaching at Quipper and Pahamify

Before I even graduated, I was teaching. It was my first exposure to the corporate world. Surprisingly, it was also the first time I tried to climb it 😂

At Quipper I was an academic coach. I gave students one-on-one online consultations and weekly study plans, and ran webinars on getting into university. Many of my students kept coming back, which meant a lot to me.

At Pahamify I taught physics for university entrance exams, live and through self-study material. On Twitter I was @fisikaambis. Later I was trusted to lead the academic team.

As Academic Lead (a project manager, with more lesson plans) I led a team of teachers. We ran our learning products the agile way: videos, summaries, infographics, question banks, tryouts and live classes. I hired new teachers, planned the live classes and introduced a brainstorming framework that made our content better.

I never really stopped teaching. For years, on the side, I taught calculus, physics, Python and C++, thermodynamics and circuits to university students, prepared high schoolers for UTBK, SAT, ACT, A-Level and IB, and coached olympiad teams. I worked with Excelsis Learning Center, Kejarcita, Kabisa Education, Generasi Cerdas Indonesia, Schoters and several schools.

Stop 3 · Jakarta

Into the banking world at BCA

a quick detour: for one month I was a software engineer at CAD-IT Consultants, building a web app with ThingWorx to control and monitor IoT products. Then the bank called.

I joined Bank Central Asia (BCA), one of Indonesia's largest banks, as a Product Manager for credit cards.

It was a quite unique blend of IT x Finance x Project Management. I led credit card digital features from idea to launch, with a team of business analysts, developers and testers, reporting to the VP.

For every feature I ran the numbers first, financial projections and cost-benefit analysis, to win senior management's approval. Then I drew the system architecture, the data flows, the screens and the logic. Risk, compliance, legal, security and audit all had a say, and so did Bank Indonesia and the OJK, Indonesia's financial regulator. Outside the bank I worked with Visa, Mastercard, American Express, JCB, UnionPay and our technology partners.

I learned how money actually moves, and how much careful work it takes to keep it moving safely.

Stop 4 · Back to hard engineering

Power System Engineer at Merz, then Mott MacDonald

Then I missed the hard engineering. I joined Merz Consultancy, a Perth energy consultancy, as a Power System Engineer. I worked from Jakarta, alongside the managing director.

It was my first exposure to Australia: its grid, its rules and its mine sites. I ran power system studies in steady state, RMS and EMT with DIgSILENT PowerFactory, and automated them through its API. I modelled dispatch, analysed energy market data, advised on renewable investments and optimised designs, sometimes deterministically, sometimes stochastically. When the software we had could not do what we needed, I helped write our own, in C, C++ and Python. And yes, I visited sites.

Stop 5 · Perth, Western Australia

Senior Economics Consultant at Mott MacDonald

Then I relocated to Perth as an Economics Consultant.

how I got the job

In Australia, an employer has to advertise a job locally before it can hire someone from overseas. My boss wrote the listing for exactly the person they wanted: a power system engineer who had also worked in finance and banking.

Luckily, that sounded a lot like me. I'm still grateful they took the chance.

Now I'm a Senior Economics Consultant. I do the commercial and the technical work: project and bid management, strategic advice for the energy industry, techno-economic modelling and power system analysis. Clients include miners, system operators and renewable developers, mostly in Western Australia.

I work with the rulebooks every day: the WEM Rules and WEM Procedures, the Pilbara Network Rules and the NWIS Harmonised Technical Rules, with some time in the NEM. I'm a Professional Member of Engineers Australia.

My favourite part is the people. I help graduate engineers and interns build energy models from scratch in Python and C++: security-constrained economic dispatch, renewables and battery investment decisions, whole-of-life asset management. With a bit of vibe-coding with generative AI, of course.

It has been years since then. I am a mate now. I still cannot eat Vegemite.

and now?

Outside the day job

I write about money, engineering and life on my blog, in English and Bahasa Indonesia. I build free tools for the sums I keep doing anyway, from renting versus buying a house to power system load flow.

I love planes, trains and travelling. You might have guessed that from the homepage. I speak Bahasa Indonesia and English, and a little Chinese, Japanese, Russian and French.
